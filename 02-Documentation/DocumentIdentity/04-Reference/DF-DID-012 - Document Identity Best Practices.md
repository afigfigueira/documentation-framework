# 1. Purpose

This document provides non-normative architectural recommendations for applying the Document Identity component under explicit subjects, domains and responsible owner contracts. It complements applicable Architecture, Model, Schema, Validation, Governance, Relationships, Constraints and Extensibility sources without replacing their authority.

Its advice concerns applicable assigned normalized logical documents, their identity-related records and actual proposed operations. Distinguish pre-allocation subjects, physical files, exact editions/status, other entity keys, descriptors, registrations and graph projections. Every README.md, CHANGELOG.md and ArchitecturalMilestones.md remains non-normalized at every location; file or entity presence alone is not an allocation decision.

Recommended practices support traceability, maintainability and compatible interpretation where assessed. They do not guarantee every implementation's interoperability or full consistency, create a runtime service or authorize allocation, migration, approval, publication or Baseline selection. Actual claims need identified rules, sources/editions, artifacts/context, checks, executor, observations, coverage and limits.

# 3. General Recommendations

Treat an assigned document identity as a governed logical document-key/domain association rather than merely a repository locator, generated technical string or current descriptor. Technical representations and legitimate adapters still have actual contracts and can preserve that meaning.

For applicable assigned normalized documents, recommend preserving the qualified Domain+Identifier key and same-document association through ordinary evolution, with allocated/retired reservations and historical interpretation. Mutable support records, files, exact editions and other entity-domain identities are different subjects; semantic preservation does not freeze all records.

Follow applicable owner meanings and common Architecture/Policy constraints. Ordinary compatible additions differ from necessary assessed normative revision (§10); no local advice grants permission to silently alter a governing key, transfer ownership, conceal a required cycle or retarget historical snapshots.

# 4. Identity Assignment

The three original assignment recommendations remain. Apply them to actual intended normalized document/domain subjects and responsible allocation decisions; these recommendations supply no generation algorithm or universal assignment time.

---

## 4.1. Assign Identity Once

Recommend one governing assignment for the applicable logical document under its domain contract, with actual allocation/association authority and collision/reservation/history evidence. Architectural recognition, a file's creation, registration or a generated header is not by itself assignment.

Ordinary content, metadata, classification, location, format and edition/status evolution should preserve the assigned key. Copies and exact editions of the same document do not automatically need new identities. A genuinely different logical document needs its own applicable subject/allocation assessment; necessary governing-contract transitions follow §5.2 rather than being disguised as ordinary evolution.

---

## 4.2. Assign Identity Early

Recommend establishing an applicable document's identity early enough to support its actual traceability needs, when the responsible domain and allocation decision/evidence are available. Governance entry alone neither allocates a key nor proves authority, uniqueness or which lifecycle assertion applies.

Pre-allocation subjects and excluded functional files remain distinct. Do not invent an identity or reservation simply to satisfy an early-assignment recommendation, and do not infer an organizational role or permission from authorship, file custody or architectural naming.

---

## 4.3. Avoid Temporary Identities

Avoid presenting temporary tracking labels as canonical assigned document identities. Where provisional tracking is necessary, distinguish its subject, scope and responsible record from the governing Domain+Identifier key; no new universal lifecycle state or Metadata Element is defined here.

Canonical assignment still needs the actual responsible domain/series decision and collision/reservation/history evidence. Preserve traceable provisional-to-assigned interpretation, original records and affected reference/context mappings without silently promoting a placeholder, fabricating a past allocation or retargeting historical evidence. A matching string alone is not that decision.

# 5. Identity Preservation

The three original preservation subjects remain. Recommend preserving assigned same-document identity while separately assessing actual record, representation, reference, consumer and decision effects.

---

## 5.1. Preserve Identity During Revisions

Content revisions, corrections, restructurings, metadata and classification changes should preserve the assigned qualified key under its governing contract. Supporting state/reference/descriptor/registration/projection records and documentary Version/Revision/Status can change under their own responsible rules.

Keep exact changed artifacts, contributors, applicable source editions and observations traceable. Preserving logical identity does not automatically validate changed bytes, merge conflicting mandatory records or extend an earlier validation result to a successor.

---

## 5.2. Preserve Identity During Migration

Repository, storage and technology movement should preserve the assigned logical key through ordinary migration. Actual locators, indexes, aliases, freshness, interfaces and references can be affected; examine prior/new representations and intended targets rather than assuming the movement is harmless.

Before a necessary normative/domain/path/representation transition, recommend identifying prior/new responsible rules and actual authority, rationale/alternatives, exact affected semantic/record owners, consumers and indispensable interfaces. Assess compatibility and document exact mappings/qualification, canonical aliases, collisions/reservations/history, same-document continuity and reference/exact-edition/pinned-snapshot effects. Preserve allocated/retired non-reuse and never silently reassign a key to another document or retarget an immutable historical member.

Apply only the separately authorized scoped change with actual contribution, edition/history and transition evidence; retain original failed checks and later validate the resulting exact artifacts. Advice or a local PASS alone grants no migration or mutation. No migration is executed by this document.

---

## 5.3. Preserve Identity During Publication

Different published formats or physical copies of the same logical document should preserve its governing identity rather than automatically allocate additional document keys. Exact editions, representations and publication records remain distinguishable with their source/context evidence.

Publication, formal approval and Baseline membership are separate decisions. A Draft successor does not inherit received Canonical naming, approval or selection into Baseline1.0. Preserve immutable historical exact-edition selections and relevant pinned relation context; a current path or stable identity alone does not identify an exact approved member.

# 6. Identifier Management

The three original identifier-management subjects remain. Recommend responsible qualified interpretation and stable assigned allocation, not a context-free string convention or running resolver.

---

## 6.1. Prefer Stable Identifiers

Preserve the assigned canonical identifier interpretation and same-document association through ordinary evolution under its governing Domain+Identifier contract. Distinguish that key from a canonical rendering, location, alias, descriptor or exact edition.

For this corpus the domain is Documentation Framework, represented by DF. DF-SSS-NNN uses a registered uppercase three-letter stable allocation series and positive three-digit001–999 allocation. 000 is not allocated; allocated/retired numbers remain reserved, and gaps prove no availability. At999, register a traceable unused continuation series rather than widen syntax or recycle. Current Family/Type/Component/path/status/edition does not reconstruct an assigned key. Other domains need their own explicit contracts.

Actual uniqueness needs responsible canonicalization, assignments, population/collision/reservation/history and coverage evidence. Syntax or a partial index is not proof of global uniqueness or availability. Necessary governing-contract transitions use §5.2; this source allocates or renumbers no key.

---

## 6.2. Avoid Semantic Encoding

Avoid deriving a governing key from values expected to change. The five original examples remain:

- repository names;
- storage locations;
- workflow states;
- file paths;
- implementation technologies.

Current mutable Family/Type/Component and metadata values likewise should not regenerate an assigned key. A registered stable series may retain a recorded historical origin or purpose without encoding the document's current classification. Do not renumber an existing valid identifier merely because that historical series code is meaningful. Domain prefixes and legitimate canonical representations remain governed by their responsible contracts, not rejected merely as semantic encoding.

---

## 6.3. Maintain Identifier Consistency

Recommend consistent intended qualified document identity and canonical interpretation across document-targeting references. Exact-edition references additionally need the responsible coordinates/bytes and context. Other endpoint kinds follow their own typed identity contracts; not every architectural reference targets a document.

Legitimate aliases, alternative representations and adapters need logical/domain mappings, responsible source/edition provenance and actual interface compatibility; they are not solely implementation design choices. Repeated renderings of one intended key are not alone competing allocations.

Incoming references may be0..N; optional incoming population does not waive validity of each actual reference. Record actual alias/target interpretation and dangling, ambiguous, stale or unavailable observations. Historical interpretation is distinct from current connection validity and live retrieval. Never silently create a key or retarget an immutable historical member to make a reference appear valid; no perpetual resolver is promised.

# 7. Cross-Component Integration

The five original integration subjects remain. Distinguish identity semantic ownership under common Architecture/Policy from actual descriptor/category/connection/registration/projection owners and other entity domains. Documentary authority, required consumption, projection, navigation and execution have different meanings.

---

## 7.1. Metadata

Metadata should associate descriptors with the intended applicable document or exact edition/context without redefining document-key meaning. Its Dictionary/Schema/Profile/Instance and other subject/entity contracts remain separate. Traceable representation of identity values, with responsible source/edition provenance, is not itself prohibited duplication or a competing allocation.

Metadata→Document Identity is the actual required identification claim for the applicable normalized document described. The functional DID README also states: “The Document Identity component depends on the Metadata component for the canonical definition of metadata concepts used to describe document identity.” Current DID-009§5.1.1/§10 retains both claims. Recommend keeping these actual reciprocal requirements explicit pending responsible core-versus-descriptor/interface/source-edition reconciliation; projection or navigation relabelling does not dissolve a genuine required cycle.

No absent Metadata Element/Profile, mandatory state or approval fact is invented by representing identity information.

---

## 7.2. Classification

Classification should categorize the intended document under its own category/assignment contracts and consume the applicable document-key meaning without redefining it. Category/definition/entity keys are not automatically normalized document IDs. Changes to current classification labels do not reconstruct an assigned stable document series/key.

---

## 7.3. Registry

Registry entries should identify their intended qualified document or exact edition/context with source provenance and freshness limits. Registration/index records retain Registry ownership; internal entry keys and locators do not replace canonical document identity.

Repeated entries or copied key values are not alone duplicate allocations. Registration is distinct from allocation, approval and completeness of population/history checks. No mandatory universal Registry intermediary or running lookup service is implied.

---

## 7.4. Relationships

Relationships should preserve typed connection/endpoints and provenance under its owner contracts. Document endpoints use the intended qualified document/exact-edition context in §6.3; other endpoint types keep their own identity rules.

An actual connection still needs its applicable type/direction/subject/context and observed validity; canonical target identity alone does not establish the whole connection's correctness or permanent retrieval. Connection changes do not allocate identity, authorize silent target replacement or redefine the document key.

---

## 7.5. Knowledge Graph

Document nodes should preserve intended qualified document-key meaning while Knowledge Graph owns logical node/edge projection and mutable properties. Source identity and Relationships connection records remain authoritative with actual edition/context provenance and refresh limits.

Knowledge Graph→Document Identity for document nodes and Knowledge Graph→Relationships for typed connections are selected required contracts, not removed because data is projected. Logical representation/interface requirements are architectural responsibilities; storage/runtime choices remain implementation concerns. Derived records do not establish competing authority, new allocation, synchronized consumers or complete graph/acyclicity proof.

Applicable DMS, tool or manual processes execute actual operations, with CMS governing changes in its scope.

Approval workflows, reviews, publication, archival and repository movement need actual responsible rules/editions, authority/decision, exact subject/context, executor and observations. Authorship, custody, received Canonical status or a local PASS alone grants no operational permission. Document Status, file/edition/index deletion or archiving does not automatically allocate/retire the logical identity or grant an unlisted DID-006 transition.

Baselines needs exact eligible approved editions, actual selection and relevant pinned relationship context or traceable verification evidence. Logical identity, publication copies and validation results alone select no member. Draft successors do not inherit approval/membership or retarget immutable history. Identity lifecycle, document edition/Status, review, validation, approval, publication and Baseline inclusion remain distinct.

Recommend preserving original observations/failed checks, obtaining actual responsible authority for a scoped change, recording owner/consumer impact, contributor/version/history and later exact validation as in §5.2. A result does not approve/publish a successor or select Baseline membership; changed inputs/rules/context need their own assessment.

# 11. Common Anti-Patterns

The seven original anti-pattern labels remain advisory cautions. An actual mandatory violation needs the responsible applicable rule/edition, intended subject/domain/context, actual observed assignment/record/event and coverage. A label, repeated string, suspected issue or missing history alone is not a verdict or repair permission. Preserve original evidence; separately authorized remediation and later exact validation follow §5.2.

---

## 11.1. Multiple Identities

Avoid competing assignments/canonical interpretations for the same logical document contrary to its governing document/domain contract. Copies, legitimate aliases, exact editions, repeated registrations and graph projections of one intended key are not automatically multiple logical allocations. Actual prior/current subject and assignment evidence determines the applicable observation; do not erase history or invent missing allocation facts.

---

## 11.2. Identity Reuse

Avoid reassigning allocated or retired keys to a different logical document. Preserve original association, actual retirement scope/decision, reservations and history. Missing index records or unavailable content is neither availability nor proof an observed reassignment occurred. Gaps do not authorize reuse, and advice does not execute retirement or allocation.

---

## 11.3. Mutable Identifiers

Avoid arbitrary or nonconforming changes to the assigned qualified key/same-document association. Mutable state/reference/descriptor/registration/projection records and legitimate aliases/representations are not all identifier mutations. Necessary governing-contract/domain/representation transitions first need §5.2 responsible prior/new authority, owner/consumer, compatibility/history/reference/snapshot assessment; no blanket arbitrary mutation is recommended.

---

## 11.4. Repository-Dependent Identities

Avoid reconstructing a document key from mutable repository paths, folders or custody. Repository locators, internal entry keys and source-control representations can legitimately change or map to an existing key under their contracts. Preserve assigned identity and actual prior/new references/index freshness/history; such adapters are not automatically repository-dependent competing semantics.

---

## 11.5. Technology-Dependent Identities

Avoid letting implementation choices silently redefine governing document-key/domain meaning or derive unstable keys from mutable technologies. Legitimate domain-preserving serialization, language structures, storage/API mappings and explicitly governed representation/interface constraints are distinct from semantic redefinition. A registered domain prefix or stable historical series code is not automatically a violation merely because its representation has technical form.

---

## 11.6. Workflow-Dependent Identities

Avoid regenerating or switching an assigned document key merely because workflow/document Status changes. Workflow/Policies own applicable transition/rule contracts, while actual DMS/tool/manual/CMS operations require their own evidence. State/edition/result record changes do not alone alter identity; deletion or archiving alone is not logical retirement or approval of a missing transition.

---

## 11.7. Metadata-Derived Identities

Avoid deriving or regenerating governing identity from mutable metadata/category values. Current Family/Type/Component/title/status/location does not recalculate the stable allocation series/key. Metadata can legitimately represent an existing key and support allocation/evaluation evidence under responsible owners without owning identity semantics, inventing allocation or making every repeated descriptor a competing identity.
